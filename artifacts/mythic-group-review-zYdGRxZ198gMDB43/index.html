Aurodk

Blood Death Knight

Main takeaway

Your damage windows are functioning[1]. The clearest opportunities are making Raise Dead part of your routine and spending Runic Power before another builder wastes it[1]. This does not look like a tank who needs a rotation overhaul[1].

This review covers all seven completed keys in the session[1]. Partial runs are excluded from whole-key damage comparisons[1]. Times below are elapsed time within the named run[1].

1. Make the ghoul a regular damage button

You cast Raise Dead six times across seven completed keys[1]. Four were in one Kings' Rest +16 and two were in Temple +17[1]. The completed Voidscar +16 had none, including during its three substantial boss fights[1].

Your captured San'layn build includes Raise Dead, and the later Temple casts confirm that you used the button there[1]. Two verified San'layn Murder Row +17 comparisons used it 11 and eight times[9][10]. Those are examples of repeated use, not a quota for your different routes[9][10].

Simple fix: summon on the first substantial pull or boss, then look for the next meaningful use when it is available[2]. The current San'layn opener includes Raise Dead alongside the damage cooldown setup[2].

The easiest improvement is remembering the button consistently, not finding a perfect alignment and leaving it unused while waiting.

2. Spend before building into capped Runic Power

Temple's opening boss gives a clear example[1]. At approximately 3:52 to 3:53, Vampiric Strike resource gains are fully wasted while Gift of the San'layn is active[1]. Death Strike follows at about 3:53.9[1]. The same pattern appears inside Gift in Voidscar and Kings' Rest[1].

This is an active-combat sequencing issue, not simply ending a pull with a full bar[1]. These resource events can include triggered or mirrored generation; they are not a count of extra buttons you pressed[1].

Simple hook: high RP → Death Strike before another builder. Fit resource spending into the San'layn window rather than treating Vampiric Strike as a reason to stop spending[2]. Healing needs still matter[2].

I am not prescribing a fixed Vampiric Strike-to-Death Strike ratio or converting reported waste into a promised number of extra Death Strikes.

What is already working

Your final two completed keys have 14 and 15 Dancing Rune Weapons, with the damage trinket paired within about two seconds each time[1]. Actual Gift windows contain both replacement strikes and Death Strikes[1].

The two completed Kings +16 runs also show functioning cleave: every observed strike-damage group hitting at least three targets occurs inside the Death and Decay aura[1]. That does not prove perfect ground coverage, but it does not support a major cleave-failure diagnosis[1].

Next-key focus

  • Remember Raise Dead during meaningful combat.
  • Check RP before another builder, especially during Gift.

Small tanking side note: in an incomplete Murder Row +17, Bone Shield expired with eight stacks remaining shortly before a lethal melee[1]. Make its duration visible as well as its stack count[1].

Comparison logs differ in gear, pulls and dungeon duration. No expected-DPS figure or damage-gain percentage is implied. Full later talent trees were unavailable, so observed San'layn mechanics do not prove every talent stayed identical.

Gelopal

Holy Paladin

Main takeaway

You have a functioning healing-and-damage routine[1]. The main damage opportunity is reducing capped Holy Power generation when you can safely spend[1]. Wings planning varied substantially through the session; the final keys show a useful pattern to repeat[1].

This review covers all seven completed keys in the session[1]. Healing requirements and encounter objectives are part of the assessment, not something ignored to chase healer DPS[1]. Times below are elapsed within the named run[1].

1. Check Holy Power before the next generator

Explicit resource events repeatedly show generation wasted at the cap[1]. In Temple's opening pull at about 0:47, Judgment generates one wasted Holy Power, then Shield of the Righteous follows roughly a second later[1]. Earlier Kings examples also show consecutive Judgment and Holy Shock generation being wasted[1].

Simple hook: check the bar before building again.

  • If someone needs recovery, use the appropriate healing spender when safe.
  • If the party is stable and no dangerous hit is imminent, look for Shield of the Righteous before another generator.
  • Keep a healing reserve when the next damage event is uncertain[15].

This is not a quota for damage spenders[1]. Triggered generation contributes to the waste totals, and not every capped gain represents an avoidable bad button press[1].

There are already strong damage-spending examples[1]. On Merektha, you cast 26 Shields of the Righteous and six Hammers of Wrath, alongside 18 Words of Glory[1]. That is evidence that both jobs can fit into the encounter[1].

2. Give Wings a planned job

The three Kings runs have 8, 8 and 6 Avenging Wrath casts[1]. Actual Wings coverage is absent throughout every Mchimba attempt and Council kill in those runs[1]. Golden Serpent, however, gets repeated uses[1].

The final Voidscar and Temple keys each have 12 Wings casts, with active Wings coverage on all seven boss kills across those two keys[1]. That is the pattern worth making consistent[1].

Simple fix: before a boss or substantial pull, decide which opening stretch or damage event gets Wings[3]. Hold briefly for something specific, not indefinitely for an undefined emergency[3]. Frequent, deliberate Wings use supports both healing and damage[3].

A compatible Lightsmith/Faith Kings +18 comparison used Wings 16 times, but different routes and cooldown opportunities mean sixteen is not your target count[11].

What not to change

Your Lightsmith buttons are used consistently, and you already cast damaging spells during Wings[1]. Necessary healing should not be replaced with a rigid DPS sequence.

Avatar of Sethraliss is the clearest exception to damage-spender comparisons[1]. You use two Wings windows and substantial direct healing on the Avatar[1]. Healing that encounter target advances the fight; only two Shields of the Righteous there is not DPS neglect[1].

Next-key focus

  • Preserve the boss Wings plan shown in the final keys.
  • Look for safe spending before capped generation.

Small defensive side note: tie Divine Protection to predictable personal damage. That is separate from the main damage homework.

No HPS grade, healer blame or promised DPS gain is attached. Peer builds are compatible rather than identical, and full later talent trees were unavailable.

Saeda

Assassination Rogue

Main takeaway

Your sustained boss bleeds and poison-buff upkeep look reassuring[1]. The clearest damage improvement is making Deathmark and Kingsbane overlap consistently on the same durable enemy[1]. You are already pressing the cooldowns frequently; the issue is sometimes separating their useful windows[1].

This review covers all seven completed keys in the session[1]. Cooldown overlap is measured from actual outgoing debuffs on the same target and target instance[1]. Times below are elapsed within the named run[1].

1. Make Deathmark and Kingsbane meet

The three Kings runs have 4/14, 2/13 and 2/10 Deathmarks with no Kingsbane overlap[1]. Voidscar has 2/11; Temple has 6/11[1]. Not every missing overlap is automatically a mistake, particularly on short-lived trash or during encounter downtime[1].

Temple's Galvazzt supplies two clear examples at 16:55.9 and 19:11.3[1]. You continue landing damage throughout those Deathmarks, but Kingsbane occurs separately[1]. Immunity does not explain the separation in those windows[1].

You also demonstrate good execution[1]. All four Deathmarks on Voidscar's Atroxus and Charonus overlap Kingsbane, and both Dazar Deathmarks in one Kings +16 overlap a full Kingsbane window[1].

Simple hook: Deathmark → Kingsbane on the same durable target. Plan the intervening Kingsbane so the next paired window stays aligned[4]. Do not blindly hold every Kingsbane, or move the package onto an enemy about to die[4].

Two verified Assassination Murder Row +17 comparisons pair every Deathmark closely with Kingsbane[12][13]. Those players use Fatebound rather than your captured Deathstalker package, so they illustrate the shared cooldown interaction, not an entire rotation to copy[12][13].

2. Preserve the upkeep habits that support the burst

Golden Serpent's official boss windows show Garrote and Rupture present for nearly the whole encounter in all three Kings runs[1]. Final-key checks on Atroxus and Galvazzt also find the bleeds present on almost all positive-damage observations[1]. Those latter checks are event-weighted observations, not whole-key uptime percentages[1].

Envenom covers approximately 92 to 98% of observed Kingsbane time across Kings, then 95.6% in Voidscar and 91.8% in Temple[1]. This supports keeping the poison-buff habit while improving cooldown alignment; it does not prove every uncovered second was avoidable[1].

Supporting hook: bleeds established, Envenom maintained through Kingsbane. That upkeep belongs around the paired burst, not in place of it[4].

There is no supported reason here to prescribe more finisher presses, accuse you of low-combo-point finishers, or declare trash bleed spreading poor. The necessary resource snapshots and enemy-lifespan denominators were not available.

Next-key focus

Make the paired burst window deliberate. Reproduce the strong Atroxus, Charonus and Dazar examples on other durable targets.

Small survival side note: leave lethal mechanics even if burst just started, and use the appropriate defensive before unavoidable exposure. That is separate from the cooldown-overlap finding.

No DPS-loss percentage is inferred. Earlier Deathstalker talent capture and later matching effects do not prove an unchanged full talent tree.

Dreidrei

Elemental Shaman

Main takeaway

Your damage cooldowns and spenders generally function well[1]. This review does not establish a large rotational mistake that justifies rebuilding your play[1]. The useful cleanup is coordinating a few burst setups and making the spell after Lava Burst deliberate[1].

This review covers all seven completed keys in the session, with official boss windows used where needed[1]. Travel, utility and encounter downtime are not automatically treated as lost casting[1]. Times below are elapsed within the named run[1].

1. Check Stormkeeper and Ascendance together

Many setups are already coordinated[1]. Stormkeeper precedes Ascendance by approximately 0 to 3 seconds in 11 of 12 Voidscar activations[1]. Temple is less consistent at 6 of 13, but that does not mean the other seven bursts were all wrong[1]. One Temple Stormkeeper occurs shortly after Ascendance and still lands early in the burst[1].

A clearer example is Temple at 23:47.1[1]. Stormkeeper was cast at 23:32.1, its charges were consumed by Chain Lightning, and its aura ended at 23:35.3[1]. Ascendance then began after those charges were gone[1].

Simple hook: when both are available for a planned burst, prepare Stormkeeper before committing Ascendance[5]. Check the two buttons together rather than deciding on each independently[5].

This is a setup-consistency adjustment, not a rule to hold every ready cooldown indefinitely. A target about to die, the next pull or another specific encounter need can change the decision. The logs do not establish how much damage a different sequence would have gained.

2. Make the next spell after Lava Burst deliberate

With Master of the Elements, look for a suitable spender or lightning spell to consume the buff before refreshing it with another Lava Burst[5].

Simple hook: buff lit + useful empowered spell available → consume it. Do not force an unavailable spender or assume every consecutive Lava Burst is a mistake.

The session contains refreshes around immediate Earthquake consumption, utility casts and target changes[1]. Those need different judgments; a raw refresh count is not a mistake tally[1].

What is already working

Free-spender handling is reassuring. All 75 Voidscar and 83 Temple Overcharge removals match an explicit Earthquake or Elemental Blast[1]. Temple's opening pull has clear examples of Earthquake consuming the cues while you continue building[1].

Resource spending is regular, and the reported waste does not establish a standout resource problem[1]. Flame Shock checks also do not show a major coverage fault[1].

Spiritwalker's Grace is used 11 times in each final key[1]. Official boss windows show no completed rotational-cast gaps above five seconds on Atroxus, Charonus, Adderis/Aspix or Galvazzt[1]. Pauses during burrows, utility, add transitions or Avatar's healing encounter cannot fairly be called movement failures from those gaps alone[1].

Next-key focus

  • Check Stormkeeper and Ascendance together before the planned burst.
  • Make the post-Lava Burst spell a deliberate buff-consumption decision.

Small survival side note: use movement tools for forced movement, but leave dangerous ground before finishing a cast.

No expected-DPS figure or damage-gain percentage is attached. Earlier Farseer talent captures and later matching mechanics do not prove an identical full later build.

Demonology Warlock

Main takeaway

The two clearest damage habits to work on are regular Implosion usage and spending Demonic Core before defaulting to Shadow Bolt[1]. Tyrant frequency is comparatively solid[1]. Your final keys also show that the more regular Implosion habit can carry through an entire run[1].

This review covers all seven completed keys in the session, using completed casts and actual buff windows[1]. Passive item explosions are counted separately from manual Implosion[1]. Times refer to elapsed time within the named run[1].

1. Make Implosion a normal priority

Earlier completed Vale, Kings and Murder Row runs have 54, 55 and 34 manual Implosions[1][7][8]. In Murder Row, two similar-duration Diabolist comparisons have 82 and 86[1][7][8]. Different gear, key levels and pulls prevent turning their counts into your target DPS or a required cast quota[1][7][8].

The final Voidscar and Temple runs have 52 and 58, approximately 2.0 to 2.2 casts per logged minute[1]. Implosion is also being used during their boss encounters, rather than only at the end of trash packs[1].

Simple hook: ready button + six imps + a living target → look to Implode. Do not reserve it for the last seconds of the pack[6].

Your captured build includes To Hell and Back[1]. That talent produces Imp Gang Bosses from sacrificed imps and gives Implosion a purpose on bosses as well as AoE[14]. This is build-specific advice, not a universal rule for every Demo setup[14].

The later logs contain passive Isolated Implosion damage too[1]. Those explosions are not extra presses of your Implosion button[1].

2. Spend Core before reaching for Shadow Bolt

The session includes direct examples of beginning Shadow Bolt while holding three or four Core stacks[1]. Observed time at four stacks is approximately 135 seconds in Vale, 157 in the first Kings run and 197 in Murder Row[1].

The final keys record approximately 86 seconds capped in Voidscar and 92 in Temple[1]. Some Voidscar cap time is between pulls; about 75 seconds overlaps official combat windows[1]. Temple's Galvazzt alone contains 35.5 seconds at cap, making it a useful boss example to revisit[1].

Simple fix:

  • Core high, shards have room → instant Demonbolt.
  • Shards high → Hand of Gul'dan first.
  • Use Shadow Bolt to fill the appropriate gaps, not as the automatic next button[6].

You can preserve some Cores for an upcoming burst or movement without sitting at the cap indefinitely. Cap duration is time at the limit, not a count of lost procs or a promised damage loss.

What is already working

Tyrant frequency is reasonably close to the comparison locks[1]. Each final key has 21 completed Tyrants[1]. Full 25-second Argus windows average 7.75 and 7.40 Hands of Gul'dan in those runs; shortened windows are excluded[1].

Portal hook: while the actual Argus buff is active, keep generating and spending shards into Hand of Gul'dan[6]. The logs do not justify making a complicated opener overhaul your first homework.

Next-key focus

Make Implosion regular, then make Demonbolt versus Shadow Bolt a deliberate resource decision.

Small survival side note: plan defensives before predictable damage and move before finishing a cast when necessary.

Earlier full talent captures verify the Diabolist package; later matching spells and auras do not prove every talent stayed identical. No DPS-gain percentage is inferred.
